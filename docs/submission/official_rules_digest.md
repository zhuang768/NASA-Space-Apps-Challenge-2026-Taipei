## Timing and team eligibility

Editing/submission opens November 14 at 09:00 and closes November 15 at 23:59 in the registered Local Event's timezone. For Universal Event teams, the Team Owner's residence timezone applies. A confirmed Taipei Local Event team should therefore work to the Taipei deadline. Local judging/pitches can have a different schedule: confirm with the Local Lead.

All members must be registered, confirmed rather than waitlisted, and visible on the team's Members tab at the same event. Local teams need 1–6 members; Universal teams need 2–6. Each person belongs to one team and each team addresses one official challenge. New members cannot be added after submission closes. The Team Owner's selected event determines the team's event.

The formation guide says project development must not begin before the official November 14 09:00 start. It does not expect a fully mature product after two days. Preserve actual development history rather than rewriting it.

## Required submission package

The guide identifies these fields:

1. Project Name.
2. High-Level Project Summary.
3. Link to Project Demo.
4. Link to Final Project.
5. Detailed Project Description.
6. Use of Super Intelligence (SI).
7. NASA Data.
8. Space Agency Partner Data and Other References.

Complete all fields marked required in the live form. At least one NASA dataset or resource is necessary for Global Award eligibility; partner resources are encouraged. Cite all external code, text, images, tools and data, including free/open-source material, with the necessary permissions. The demo and final-project links are distinct: a website does not replace the required short demo.

The project page is the main evaluation record; the demo supports evaluation and Presentation. Explain what was built, how it works, why it matters, the actual data role and the tools used. Do not rely solely on the external website to supply the explanation.

## Demo, access and language

Use either up to seven slides, including title/intro slides, or a video of at most 30 seconds. The guide says exceeding these limits makes the project ineligible for Global Judging. Slides should identify the team, members and selected challenge and show the product/user experience and NASA-data contribution.

The submission page and linked materials must be English. A demo video may use another spoken language only with English subtitles. Keep technical detail in the final project and documentation while making the short demo understandable without prior subject knowledge.

Videos/documents are hosted externally and linked publicly, without login, permission requests or registration. Test links anonymously. Images can be uploaded/pasted into the form as JPG/PNG/GIF, up to 10 MB; videos/documents cannot be uploaded directly there. FortiGuard classification for a new project website is recommended to reduce judge-access problems, not mandatory.

## SI / AI disclosure

The guides call AI tools SI. They permit development assistance and require truthful disclosure of where/how it was used. The submission guide also says SI should not supply the team's idea of how to solve the challenge; teams should review their actual contribution history against this wording, without hiding or inventing their process.

- SI-generated images/videos need a visible generation watermark.
- SI-generated audio needs acknowledgement in description and metadata.
- SI-generated code/data need acknowledgement in description and metadata.
- Generated content must not use, contain or modify NASA branding/design elements such as logos, flags or mission identifiers.

The form's SI response is described as data collection rather than a judge-visible answer. Judges can still see generation indicators within the work and evaluate originality, intent and execution. A private form response does not replace visible/project-level acknowledgement.

Do not relabel actual satellite observations as synthetic merely because AI helped write the processing code. Identify which code, explanation, image, audio or data was generated and which numbers came from measured sources. No generated-data or branding permission is inferred from tool access.

## Submit action and safeguards

Save Progress is not submission. Use the edit lock and coordinate edits to avoid overwrites. When ready, review the terms/privacy and original-work/reference declarations, then use Submit for Judging before the deadline. Verify the success banner/confirmation. Edits and resubmissions are allowed before closing; the final submitted version is the one to rely on. Do not assume an unsent edit is reviewed.

The guide bars images, video, audio or likenesses of people under 18 in project demonstrations/submissions. Team participation by minors is a separate issue covered by guardian requirements. Eligibility provisions, including NASA's China funding-restriction review, remain governed by the terms and NASA's determination; this digest makes no individual eligibility decision.

## Judging and awards

Every judging stage uses five criteria scored 1–5: Impact, Creativity, Validity, Relevance and Presentation. NASA data should drive the work, scientific/technical feasibility should be defensible, and the story should clearly explain the challenge and contribution. A good-looking interface alone does not establish scientific validity.

Global progression: Local/Universal judging selects Global Nominees (December 3, 2026); global judging selects Finalists and Honorable Mentions (December 17); executive judging selects Global Winners (January 26, 2027). Local prizes are separate and do not automatically imply a global nomination. The guide describes a virtual winner celebration in early 2027, not a guaranteed NASA-site trip.

Ten award categories: Best Use of Science; Best Use of Data; Best Use of Technology; Galactic Impact; Best Mission Concept; Most Inspirational; Best Storytelling; Global Connection; Art & Technology; Local Impact. These are award categories, not ten additional submission sections.
