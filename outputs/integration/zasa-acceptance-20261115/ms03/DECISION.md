# MS-03: quantitative sampling bound, not a detection limit

Additional useful bound beyond PR #27's coverage check: the retained AD1 Earth vector result has median equivalent-circle diameter **18.484785 m**, n=185. A hypothetical equally sized feature spans only **3.08 nominal CTX pixels** at 6 m/pixel. The retained MOC S1700607_NA map spacing is 3.000339463 m; its north-polar stereographic inverse scale gives approximately **2.765–2.772 m ground sampling** over 57.5–58°N, or **6.67–6.68 samples** across that same diameter. These are sampling ratios, not measurements of Martian polygons and not effective resolving power. AD1 median is inherited from the retained repository table, not independently rederived here.

A simple *explicitly assumed* boundary-placement sensitivity test explains why footprint support alone cannot close the geometry comparison: if each opposite diameter edge is misplaced by up to one sample, worst-case diameter error is 2p. That is **65%** of the Earth median for nominal CTX, and **30%** for retained MOC ground spacing. Under that assumption, diameter error below 20% would require p≤1.848 m, and below 10% p≤0.924 m. This is an illustration, not a universal polygon minimum-pixel rule, formal detectability calibration, confidence interval, or measured image accuracy. Equivalent-circle diameter from area has additional boundary effects; this direct-diameter illustration does not quantify them.

**Decision:** keep morphology-population comparison unvalidated. The shortest decisive improvement is an inside-cell, independently characterized image with finer sampling and demonstrably traceable closed boundaries, followed by a fixed boundary rule and inter-annotator/scale-degradation validation. Do not turn the CTX/MOC review into an absence result or estimate unpublished Mars polygon counts. No new camera footprint claim was established in this bounded pass. Existing HiRISE product-family query results from PR #27 remain unchanged.
