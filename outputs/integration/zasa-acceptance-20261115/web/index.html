Where on northern Mars is shallow ice worth a closer look?

Three regions between 52°N and 58°N, picked from global orbital data. This page shows what that data says about each one, and where it stops being able to tell us anything.

These are research targets. They are not confirmed ice detections, and they are not landing sites.

What orbital data says about each region

Every value is an average over a review area of roughly 30 by 16 km. Each row is a different instrument at a different resolution, so the rows are not added into a score.

Dust haze through the Martian year

Column dust opacity at the surface, averaged over each quarter of the year. Higher means hazier.

Temperature swing between night and day

Median air temperature from the Mars Climate Sounder, in kelvin. Each line runs from night to day.

Bins are 5 degrees wide, so they sit near each region, not on it. Day and night are time-adjusted to 3 a.m. and 3 p.m. local time.

What we can and cannot say

The data is good at telling regions apart. It is not good at telling us what is on the ground.

Known at the scale of a review area

How flat each region is, how quickly its surface heats and cools, how consistent the ice evidence is at two depths, and how dusty the air gets. Thermal coverage is incomplete: 73, 93 and 79 of 100 thermal cells are measured for MS-01, MS-02 and MS-03.

Known only on a coarse map

The USGS global geologic map, drawn at 1:20 million, places MS-01 and MS-02 in the Middle Amazonian lowland unit, and MS-03 in a unit of Amazonian and Hesperian volcanic material. Those are regional labels, not descriptions of the rock or soil underfoot.

Not known

Whether ice is there, how deep, and how much. What the ground is made of, what covers it, and how rough it is at a landing scale. MS-01 and MS-02 each have a handful of 6-meter CTX images and no HiRISE images, so local detail is still out of reach.

Settling it takes projected high-resolution images, radar echoes, spectra, and an independent measurement on the ground. Earth sites such as University Valley and Haughton crater show how those methods work, but they are not matches for these regions.
